Color Contrast Gotchas

Common color combinations that look fine visually but fail WCAG AA contrast requirements — and the fixes that pass.

The Medium Blue Problem

A professional-looking blue that most people find perfectly readable with white text — but it fails AA.

✗ Fails — Ratio: 4.22:1
✓ Passes — Ratio: 4.98:1
This is our brand color. Most designers and clients prefer white text on medium blues like this, but WCAG AA requires 4.5:1 for normal text. You either need to darken the blue or switch to black text.
How WCAG 3 (APCA) Sees This Differently

How WCAG 3 (APCA) Sees This Differently

Combination WCAG 2 Ratio APCA (Lc) WCAG 2 WCAG 3
White on #2B8AC7 4.22:1 Lc 62 Fails AA Passes for 16px bold
Black on #2B8AC7 4.98:1 Lc 48 Passes AA Fails for body text

This is one of APCA's most dramatic differences. WCAG 2 treats contrast as symmetric — it doesn't care whether the light color is the text or the background. APCA accounts for polarity: light text on a dark background is actually easier to read than dark text on a light background at the same luminance difference. So white-on-blue scores higher than black-on-blue in APCA, completely flipping the WCAG 2 result.

The Orange Dilemma

Orange is one of the hardest colors to use accessibly. White text on orange almost never passes.

✗ Fails — Ratio: 2.94:1
✓ Passes — Ratio: 7.15:1
✗ Fails — Ratio: 3.00:1
✓ Passes — Ratio: 7.01:1
Orange sits in a luminance range where it's too light for white text but feels visually “wrong” with black text. This is one of the most common complaints about WCAG contrast rules. To use white text, you'd need to go so dark it barely reads as orange anymore.
How WCAG 3 (APCA) Sees This Differently

How WCAG 3 (APCA) Sees This Differently

Combination WCAG 2 Ratio APCA (Lc) WCAG 2 WCAG 3
White on #E67E22 2.94:1 Lc 55 Fails AA Passes for 18px bold
Black on #E67E22 7.15:1 Lc 67 Passes AA Passes for body text

Orange is where APCA offers the most relief. WCAG 2's symmetric formula heavily penalizes orange with white because orange has high relative luminance. APCA recognizes that white text on a medium-tone background is more readable than the simple ratio suggests. While white-on-orange still doesn't pass for small body text in APCA, it clears the bar for larger or bolder text — something WCAG 2 doesn't allow until 18pt/24px.

The Magic Gray: #767676

The lightest possible gray that passes 4.5:1 contrast on a white background. One shade lighter and it fails.

✓ Passes — Ratio: 4.54:1

#767676 on White
The absolute threshold. This is the lightest gray you can use for body text on a white background.

✗ Fails — Ratio: 4.48:1

#777777 on White
Just one step lighter and it fails. This is why #767676 is famous in accessibility circles.

✓ Passes — Ratio: 4.54:1

White on #767676
It works in reverse too. White text on this gray background also passes at exactly 4.54:1.

✓ Passes — Ratio: 4.62:1

Black on #767676
Black text on this gray also passes, making #767676 one of the few colors that works with both black and white text.

#767676 is the accessibility community's most famous hex code. It represents the exact tipping point where gray meets the AA threshold against white. It's a useful benchmark when choosing gray tones for placeholder text, icons, or secondary content.
How WCAG 3 (APCA) Sees This Differently

How WCAG 3 (APCA) Sees This Differently

Combination WCAG 2 Ratio APCA (Lc) WCAG 2 WCAG 3
#767676 on White 4.54:1 Lc 56 Just passes AA Fails for body text
White on #767676 4.54:1 Lc 61 Just passes AA Passes for 16px bold

In WCAG 2, these two combinations have the exact same ratio because the formula is symmetric. APCA breaks that symmetry. Dark text on a light background needs a higher Lc value to pass than light text on a dark background at the same font size, because our eyes process the two polarities differently. So the famous #767676 actually fails APCA for regular body text on white — you'd need closer to #5e5e5e (Lc 75) for comfortable 16px reading. The “magic gray” loses its magic under the new algorithm.

Red Alert Buttons

Bright reds used for error states and destructive actions often fail with white text.

✗ Fails — Ratio: 3.99:1
✓ Passes — Ratio: 5.27:1
✓ Passes — Ratio: 5.73:1
✓ Passes — Ratio: 3.66:1
Bright reds like Bootstrap's classic #E74C3C fail with white text. You need to darken to at least around #C0392B for white text to pass. But notice: as the red gets dark enough for white, black text starts to lose contrast.

The Green Paradox

Green is deceptively tricky. “Success green” buttons almost always fail with white text.

✗ Fails — Ratio: 2.31:1
✓ Passes — Ratio: 9.10:1
✗ Fails — Ratio: 3.25:1
✓ Passes — Ratio: 6.47:1
Green has very high luminance, which means white text almost never works on it at any saturation. To pass with white text, you'd need something like #1B7A3D — which most people wouldn't call “green” anymore. This is arguably the single most frustrating color in WCAG compliance.
How WCAG 3 (APCA) Sees This Differently

How WCAG 3 (APCA) Sees This Differently

Combination WCAG 2 Ratio APCA (Lc) WCAG 2 WCAG 3
White on #2ECC71 2.31:1 Lc 44 Fails AA Fails
White on #27AE60 3.25:1 Lc 53 Fails AA Fails for body text
White on #1B7A3D 5.14:1 Lc 68 Passes AA Passes for 16px bold

Green is the one color where APCA largely agrees with WCAG 2. Green's high luminance genuinely does make white text hard to read. APCA actually makes it slightly harder to pass because it accounts for the fact that our eyes are most sensitive to green light, so a green background produces more visual “noise” against white text than you'd expect from luminance alone. Green remains the toughest color for accessible white text in both standards.

Placeholder Text Trap

The default placeholder gray in most browsers fails contrast. And many designs make it even worse.

✓ Passes — Ratio: 4.60:1

Placeholder color: #757575

✗ Fails — Ratio: 2.79:1

Placeholder color: #A9A9A9 (common browser default)

Many designers intentionally make placeholder text light to visually distinguish it from entered text. But WCAG still requires 4.5:1 contrast for placeholder text since it conveys information. This creates a design tension between convention and compliance.

Link Color on Colored Backgrounds

A link color that passes on white may fail on a light gray background.

✗ Fails — Ratio: 3.81:1

The same link color can pass on a white background but fail on a light gray panel or card. Always check contrast against the actual background the text sits on, not just white.

WCAG 2 ratios calculated per WCAG 2.1 Success Criterion 1.4.3 (Level AA) — minimum 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text.
APCA values reference the WCAG 3 draft Accessible Perceptual Contrast Algorithm. WCAG 3 is still in development — thresholds and scoring may change before final recommendation.
